EduTech Creative Lab · University of Hyderabad

Enhancing learning with educational technology

A small open lab where I design, try out and share ways technology can make learning more visible, more responsive and more inclusive, from instant formative feedback to simulations and AI-supported assessment.

Objective of the lab

To enhance learning with EduTech: tools that help learners understand, and help teachers know their learners.

Interactive demos

Try it yourself

Two small demonstrations of how EduTech can enhance learning. Both run in your browser, and nothing you enter is saved or sent anywhere.

Demo 1 · Formative assessment

Adaptive physics check-in with confidence

Six questions on motion, force and energy. The difficulty adapts to your answers, and you say how sure you are before seeing the result. At the end you get a learner profile, not just a score.

How this enhances learning: Instant, explained feedback corrects ideas at the moment they form. Asking for confidence separates a lucky guess from real understanding, and a confident wrong answer from a simple gap. That is what a teacher needs to know to respond well.

Demo 2 · Simulation

Predict, observe, explain: projectile motion

Choose a launch speed and angle, predict how far the ball will land, then launch it. Compare your prediction with what happens and try to explain the difference.

How this enhances learning: Predicting first makes learners commit to an idea, and the simulation then tests it. Earlier trajectories stay on screen, so learners can discover patterns themselves, such as 45° giving the longest range and 30° and 60° landing at the same spot.

Ideas & resources

How EduTech enhances learning

Six ideas the lab builds on, each with something a teacher can try tomorrow.

  • Immediate feedback

    Feedback works best while the learner still remembers their reasoning. Digital tools can respond in seconds instead of days.

    Try: a two-minute exit quiz that explains each answer.

  • Retrieval practice

    Recalling knowledge strengthens it more than re-reading. Low-stakes quizzes make recall a habit.

    Try: start each class with three questions from last week.

  • Personalisation

    Adaptive tasks let each learner work where they are ready to grow, neither bored nor lost.

    Try: offer two levels of the same task and let results decide the next one.

  • Simulation and visualisation

    Seeing a process change as you control it builds intuition that a static diagram cannot.

    Try: predict, observe, explain with a free simulation.

  • Learner data, used with care

    Data on progress, confidence and engagement helps teachers notice who needs support, if it is read alongside knowledge of the child.

    Try: ask learners to rate their confidence with each answer.

  • AI as a thinking partner

    AI can generate examples, questions and first-draft feedback. Teachers check, adapt and decide.

    Try: ask an AI for three misconceptions about a topic, then design questions that reveal them.
